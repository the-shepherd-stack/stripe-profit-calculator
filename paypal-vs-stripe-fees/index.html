PayPal vs Stripe fees for digital products: wallet reach vs margin control

PayPal can lift buyer trust and wallet conversion for some audiences. Stripe gives you tighter control over checkout logic, subscriptions, affiliates, and the post-purchase flow. Your margin math is wrong if you compare PayPal vs Stripe fees using only the headline processor rate and skip refunds, discounting, support overhead, and delivery cost.

Quick comparison framework

  • Buyer trust: PayPal can help when your audience prefers wallet checkout or already expects a PayPal button.
  • Wallet conversion: PayPal may reduce friction for impulse buyers who want a saved-payment flow instead of card entry.
  • Checkout control: Stripe usually wins if you want tighter control over pricing tests, subscriptions, and partner flows.
  • Chargeback friction: Both processors create disputes, but your real cost includes refund handling, support load, and policy edge cases.

PayPal vs Stripe fees on a $25, $50, and $100 digital sale

Using current US headline rates from the official pricing pages: Stripe domestic cards at 2.9% + $0.30 and PayPal Checkout at 3.49% + $0.49.

  • $25 sale: Stripe takes $1.03. PayPal Checkout takes $1.36. Stripe leaves $0.33 more.
  • $50 sale: Stripe takes $1.75. PayPal Checkout takes $2.24. Stripe leaves $0.49 more.
  • $100 sale: Stripe takes $3.20. PayPal Checkout takes $3.98. Stripe leaves $0.78 more.

That gap is only the processor line item. Once you add VAT, coupons, refund reserve, product cost, and support time, the platform choice becomes a fuller margin decision.

When the headline fee is not the real margin story

  • Simple domestic sale: on a $50 digital product with $5 of delivery cost and no VAT, Stripe keeps $43.25 while a PayPal Checkout-style fee stack keeps $42.76.
  • Coupon + VAT + refunds: on a $100 sale with a 10% coupon, 20% VAT, 5% refund reserve, and $5 of product cost, Stripe keeps $63.34 while the higher-fee wallet path keeps $62.62.
  • The pattern: the headline fee difference stays small in raw dollars, but the lower-margin your business already is, the faster that small difference compounds across discounts, taxes, refund reserve, and paid acquisition.

International fees, refunds, and disputes change the answer fast

  • Stripe international cards: the public pricing page adds +1.5% for international cards and +1% for currency conversion when it applies.
  • PayPal international commercial transactions: PayPal adds +1.50% on top of the domestic commercial rate.
  • Dispute cost: Stripe lists a $15 dispute fee. PayPal can win on buyer trust for some sellers, but the support burden, refund workflow, and policy complexity can erase the conversion win if you are selling globally.
  • International edge case: at $100 with VAT, refund reserve, and product cost, a Stripe path with international + FX add-ons can keep $68.46 while a PayPal international checkout assumption can keep $68.68. Once cross-border costs appear, the simple domestic fee table stops being enough.

Use Stripe, PayPal, or both?

Use Stripe if you need control

Stripe wins when you want cleaner subscription billing, better pricing experiments, stronger affiliate logic, or direct ownership of checkout and post-purchase flows.

Use PayPal if wallet trust clearly lifts conversion

PayPal wins when your audience already expects it and the faster checkout meaningfully raises completed purchases enough to offset the extra fee drag.

Use both if you want the cleanest answer from live data

Offer both when you can, then compare actual take rate, refund rate, and support load. The right answer is not the posted fee table. It is which path leaves more money after the full stack hits the sale.

What to compare before you choose

When PayPal wins

PayPal wins when faster buyer recognition matters more than deeper checkout control. If your audience already trusts PayPal, the conversion lift can offset some fee pressure.

When Stripe wins

Stripe wins when you want cleaner subscription billing, better pricing experiments, or direct ownership of the checkout and post-purchase flow. That control matters once you start testing bundles, coupons, and lifecycle automation.

How to sanity-check the Stripe side

Run your actual digital-product scenario through the calculator before you decide. Compare the resulting net profit, break-even price, and target-margin price against whatever conversion or trust advantage you think PayPal adds.
